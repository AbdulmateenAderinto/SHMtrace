60 para :: In the mOrange study[@perezduran2012], inactive-AID controls calibrated active AID across empty-vector, Ugi and UNG contexts (Figure 7E). SHMtrace retained 115 substitutions, all at G:C, with 75–100% at WRC/GYW hotspots. Active/inactive separation was 20-, 1,002- and 103-fold, respectively; Ugi zeros used one-event values. Ugi-to-UNG transition shares fell from 95% to 4%. The paired mean difference was 92 percentage points (95% CI 74–110; paired t, p = 0.0021); few calls per library limit precision.

61 para :: Two engineered-diversification datasets extended the evaluation to mutations measured relative to experimental starting constructs (Figure S18). In a study using a common GFP substrate[@liu2018], no-editor libraries yielded at most one call in 20,000 reads. AID substitutions were more frequently located at WRC/GYW hotspots than APOBEC3A substitutions, both in Wt cells (56% versus 14%; p = 0.0040) and in UNG/MSH2-deficient cells (37% versus 20%; p = 9.0 × 10^{−4}). In the repair-deficient background, however, their transition shares were similar (78% versus 76%; p = 0.74), showing that transition enrichment alone could not distinguish the two enzymes.

62 para :: The second engineered dataset, a CRISPR-X antibody-evolution study, diversified an antibody V_{H} and published the mutations it found in each selected cluster[@devilder2019]. Comparing the two lists position by position, SHMtrace recovered 150 of the 150 published mutations and called none the authors did not, across all 42 cluster entries (Figure S18B). Because the comparator is an independently curated list rather than another run of the same software, this is the sharpest available test of variant identity on this route.

63 para :: Two further datasets tested transfer to another laboratory's switch-region libraries and to a fourth platform, and showed where a deposit limits what can be asked. Sμ libraries under PI3Kδ inhibition[@compagno2017] confound genotype with library layout: six of nine wild-type libraries are single-end whereas every *Aicda*^{−/−} library is paired, and the mates do not overlap, so only the twelve paired libraries reach the switch repeats where AID acts (Figure S19). Analysed over those repeats, the nine *Aicda*^{−/−} libraries returned one call between them against 81–172 per wild-type library (p = 0.0021), and the wild-type calls carried the AID signature: targeting index 1.52–1.67 and 31–35% C→T or G→A. The calibration therefore transfers to an independent laboratory, platform and perturbation. The drug comparison does not follow from this deposit, because only one wild-type replicate per treatment reaches the repeats.

64 para :: PacBio Sμ–Sγ1 junctions[@vincendeau2022] tested junction structure rather than calibrated mutation calls (Figure S20). Mean microhomology was 1.01 nt in Wt and 1.02 nt in *Shld1*^{−/−}, rising to 1.69 nt without XLF and 1.59 nt in double mutants. Double mutants showed more ≥4-nt microhomology and fewer blunt junctions; *Shld1*^{−/−} showed more insertions. These patterns support recovery of a known repair phenotype. Counts reflect PCR recovery rather than switching efficiency; 19–47% of junctions had alternative equally good acceptor placements (STAR Methods).

65 heading :: Discussion

66 para :: Measuring somatic hypermutation has long meant choosing between reading molecules accurately and reading enough of them. This study supports a cloning-free SHM workflow by estimating technical background from controls. SHMtrace connects delivered reads or chromatograms to mutation profiles and comparisons across biological replicates. Rather than relying on quality scores alone, it estimates the background in the AID-null libraries that SHM studies already include, at the level of substitution type, strand and base quality, and checks that calibration by holding out each control in turn. Instead of treating reads as replicates, it tests independent biological samples. Both replacements held: 81% of planted J_{H}4 mutations and 90% on N114 V_{H} were recovered at false-discovery proportions near the default target, while a quality-based caller either missed most true mutations or, made permissive, called as much background as the uncorrected rule, and pooling reads produced false positives in more than 90% of simulated experiments without a true difference. Together, these results support a practical route from deeply sequenced amplicons to interpretable SHM measurements across biological replicates.

67 para :: The gap this fills is specific, and it sits between two mature bodies of software. Repertoire toolkits identify and summarise mutations well: IgBLAST assigns germlines[@ye2013], Change-O and SHazaM quantify load[@gupta2015], SHMTool compares datasets[@maccarthy2009], and S5F models targeting[@yaari2013]. They begin, however, from mismatches already taken to be real, which is why counting every mismatch to the germline separated AID from empty-vector N114 libraries by 2-fold where SHMtrace separated them by 188-fold. General-purpose variant callers do the opposite: they decide what is real, but from error models fitted to data of a different kind. LoFreq reads base qualities[@wilm2012]; ClairS and DeepSomatic were developed for tumour–normal sequencing, with different training data and calling assumptions[@zheng2026,@park2026]. The evaluated configurations did not use AID-null calibration in the manner tested here. SHMtrace closes that circuit by making the AID-null control, where available, the source of the error model, and by carrying the measurement through to the animal-level comparison that the biology requires[@lazic2010]. Its distinctive contribution is to connect control-informed mutation detection with interpretable profiles and comparisons across biological replicates in one reproducible workflow.

68 para :: Seen this way, comparator performance is a matter of operating range rather than quality. LoFreq was designed for deep short-read data with calibrated qualities; its base-alignment correction suppressed nearly all nanopore calls here, and disabling it still limited recovery of low-support variants. That is not a defect on its intended data: the configurations tested here did not capture this assay's background as effectively as matched-control calibration. The measurements say why. Above Q40, control error stopped decreasing and differed across the evaluated datasets (1–9 × 10^{−5}; Figure S2), so the quality score runs out of information exactly where SHM calling needs it. ClairS and DeepSomatic recovered clean variants at higher allele fractions but few real SHM calls, and chemistry and model mismatches further qualify external-data comparisons. Clair3 and DeepVariant found none of the planted mutations, as expected of callers built to genotype a sample against a reference rather than to find a variant carried by a few reads. Geneious provided greater single-read recall on J_{H}4 and *Pim1* but more false calls on each amplicon. These are choices a study can make deliberately: sensitive mode raises the empirical target to 0.20 and recovers 72% of single-read mutations on J_{H}4 and 87% on N114 V_{H} at observed FDR 0.18 and 0.16, while events unresolved individually can be estimated in aggregate. Together, these outputs make the sensitivity–background tradeoff explicit and help investigators choose a calling regime appropriate to their experiment.

69 para :: A shared framework accommodates four platforms through distinct analyses: control-calibrated read-library substitutions, direct Sanger counting and PacBio junction characterization. A Sanger clone is one molecule read once, so its mutations are read directly; the workflow therefore counts quality-filtered substitutions directly and reports the control-derived background separately. Nanopore and Illumina libraries carry matched AID-null arms, so each candidate is tested against the errors those controls make. Nanopore spans the whole amplicon, which made the 3′ decline visible without tiling, identified clones from the V(D)J junction and ordered their mutations into trees; Illumina gives depth without that linkage. PacBio combines length with per-base accuracy, and reading the donor, the join and the acceptor on one molecule recovered a known end-joining phenotype from a deposit with no AID-null arm, using junction shape in place of calibrated calls. Figure S21 sets out what each measured. Agreement between platforms is itself evidence: nanopore and Sanger reads from the same six mice gave substitution spectra correlated at r = 0.92 and the same AID-null contrast, supporting the use of cloning-free long reads to recover established SHM patterns while adding whole-amplicon linkage.

70 para :: Reanalysis of public data tested SHMtrace against independent biological and counting expectations. Published sequence collections support reproducible reanalysis when clones are linked to animals and genotypes: SHMtrace recovered the principal J_{H}4 frequency and G:C comparisons, matched SW_{HEL} counts across five arms and twelve substitution types (2,804 of 2,809 tabulated mutations; r = 1.0000), and recovered 150 of 150 mutations an antibody-evolution study curated by hand, calling none its authors did not. Agreement was not complete, and the exceptions are as informative: Wt frequencies differed after denominator harmonisation, the published *Mkln1* A:T decrease was not significant in the reanalysis, and the SW_{HEL} total-mutation inference differed under a different test (Tables S4 and S5; Figures S14–S16). The Sanger re-analysis also surfaced bookkeeping problems, which SHMtrace now checks and reports rather than silently resolving. By retaining assignments, counting conventions and quality flags, SHMtrace makes legacy sequence collections easier to revisit, compare and reuse.

71 para :: What this enables in practice is a short experiment-to-answer path that keeps its own evidence: read accounting, fixed-seed sampling and one engine behind the desktop and command-line interfaces, with every exclusion, threshold and flagged site written out for inspection. The power analysis sets the honest expectation. How many animals a study needs depends on the difference it seeks: under variation estimated from three Wt J_{H}4 mice, the AID-competent against AID-null contrasts made here separate at three animals per group, whereas a two-fold difference between two AID-proficient groups would require approximately twenty, with considerable uncertainty in that estimate (Figure S8B). Sequencing depth cannot substitute for independent biological samples, and no analysis can recover a comparison the design did not support. Within that boundary the framework extends readily. It needs only a target, matched AID-null libraries and a sample sheet, supporting its application to off-target loci, switch regions, engineered diversification and base-editor substrates across twelve loci from eleven laboratories. Further validation on independently prepared runs and UMI-tagged SHM libraries can establish how far this framework extends. SHMtrace brings control-calibrated mutation detection, whole-amplicon profiles and comparisons across biological replicates into one reproducible workflow. By preserving mutation linkage and documenting analytical choices, it makes long-read SHM data more informative and supports transparent reanalysis of existing sequence collections. This provides a practical foundation for studying how AID targeting and DNA repair shape antibody diversification.

72 heading :: Limitations of the study

73 para :: SHMtrace relies on representative AID-null controls prepared and sequenced alongside the samples. Error profiles can vary between runs and basecalling configurations, and calibration using controls from a different run was not evaluated. With one nanopore run per assay, the present results do not establish performance across independent runs.

74 para :: The reported variants remain subject to technical uncertainty. Without UMIs, early PCR errors, recombination and repeated sequencing of PCR-derived molecules cannot be reliably distinguished from genuine mutations or expanded biological lineages. Mutation trees therefore describe relationships among reads rather than independently established cellular lineages. The default ≥50% allele-frequency filter can also remove genuine mutations shared by a dominant lineage. Excluded sites are reported for review, and experiments with a known starting sequence can use an optional reference policy that retains high-frequency differences. Single-read mutations that cannot be called individually are estimated only in aggregate, and unique-variant densities require comparison at matched sequencing depth.

75 para :: The empirical false-discovery estimate depends on the available controls and can exceed its nominal target when background differs between libraries. Noisy-control exclusion requires at least three controls, and its effect should be reviewed. Sample-specific artifacts absent from the controls may still pass the test; spectrum and clustering flags can identify unusual patterns but require interpretation. At weakly mutated targets such as Pim1, signal remained close to background.

76 para :: The core validation used three animals per group for J_{H}4 and one locus per cell system. Settings were developed using these libraries and applied to public datasets without dataset-specific retuning, although the noisy-control exclusion rule was introduced after inspection of the Ramos results. Evaluation on an independent sequencing run with new biological samples would provide a stronger test of generalizability. Indel validation comprised nanopore spike-ins and one Illumina biological phenotype, while UMI validation used rRNA rather than SHM data. These experiments do not establish performance on biological nanopore indels or UMI-tagged SHM libraries; molecule-level consensus remains preferable when available. Two Sanger studies originated from the authors’ laboratory, while the independent Sanger study provided sequences without chromatograms or AID-null controls. Together, these datasets evaluate distinct parts of the workflow, with different limits on the conclusions each can support.

126 heading :: Read preparation

127 para :: SHMtrace reads FASTQ files, which may be gzipped or, for Illumina data, paired files that are merged on their overlap. Reads are then processed in the following order. First, reads longer than a locus-specific limit (2,000 bp for J_{H}4 and 900 bp for N114 V_{H}) are removed, as are reads with a mean expected error above 0.01 per base, calculated from Phred qualities[@edgar2015]. Second, reads are aligned with minimap2 (map-ont preset, or sr for Illumina) against an index containing the target and any competing references, and each read is assigned to the sequence it aligns to best. Unmapped reads, reads assigned to a competing reference, reads with a supplementary alignment (chimeras), and reads aligned over less than a minimum length (800 bp for J_{H}4 and 400 bp for N114 V_{H}) are removed. For J_{H}4, the competing references were a J_{H}4-like paralog (the consensus of Agouti-type clones, approximately 12% divergent) and a recurrent contaminant haplotype. Because a full-length paralog read can align better to the longer target, reads carrying 10 or more bases specific to a competing reference, at sites where fewer than 20% of the sample's reads carry them, are also removed. Genuine J_{H}4 reads carry 0–7 such bases, whereas paralog reads and chimeras carry 11–62. The fate of every read is recorded. Usable reads were then downsampled at random with a fixed seed (Python random, seed 0) to the analysis depth: 500 reads for J_{H}4 and N114 V_{H} and 2,000 reads for the IRCM loci. The analysis windows were positions 101–629 (529 bp, the window shared with Sanger) or 90–1,230 (full length) of the J_{H}4 reference and positions 21–635 of the N114 V_{H} reference. For loci whose window is not set by a preset, it runs from position 21 to 20 bases before the reference's end: Sμ 21–631 (611 bp), *Pim1* 21–882 (862 bp), off-target 1 21–988 (968 bp) and off-target 2 21–962 (942 bp).

128 heading :: Control-calibrated variant calling

129 para :: Each observed single-nucleotide difference within the window is a candidate, including substitutions supported by one read. Control error rates are estimated by substitution type, strand and quality threshold, combining a pooled rate with position-specific counts. For a candidate with k supporting reads ranked q_{1} ≥ q_{2} ≥ …, the test evaluates, for each j ≤ k, the probability of at least j errors with quality ≥qj, using strand-specific depth and control rates multiplied by margin c. The reported p value is the smallest tail probability multiplied by k.

130 para :: The margin c is chosen by leaving one control out at a time. Each control is tested against an error model built from the other controls, and c is the smallest value in the grid 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, and 50 at which no more than 1% of candidate tests pooled across held-out controls reach p < 0.01. In the analyses reported here, c was 5× on J_{H}4, 10× on the full length, and 10× on N114 V_{H}. The same held-out tests, made at the samples' analysis depth, are then used to set the significance threshold. Each held-out control is kept as a decoy, defined by its sorted p values and the number of bases it tested. For a sample and a candidate threshold t, the expected number of false calls is the mean, across decoys, of the number of decoy p values ≤ t, scaled by the ratio of the sample's tested bases to the decoy's when that ratio exceeds 1. Decoys are never scaled down to a shallower library, whose coarser p values they would otherwise underestimate. SHMtrace keeps the largest t at which the expected number of false calls is at most 0.05 times the number of sample p values ≤ t, an empirical false-discovery estimate inspired by target–decoy analysis[@elias2007]. A control is never used as its own decoy: when a control is tested, only the other controls serve as decoys. Each sample is judged against the controls of its own sequencing run when that run has at least two (one, for a control), and against all controls otherwise, with the report naming any sample whose run has no control; every J_{H}4 and N114 V_{H} sample shared its controls' run, while the public deposits do not record runs, so their samples were judged against all of their study's controls. A control whose held-out candidates at p ≤ 0.01 per base exceed five times the median of the other controls, and number at least five, is left out of the decoys (unless no decoy would remain) and named in the report; it still contributes to the error model, and is never removed from the analysis. The comparison needs a majority to judge against, so it is made only when at least two other controls remain, that is with three or more controls; with exactly two, the more variable library is named in the report but not left out, because which of the two is unusual cannot be established. The rule was written for one public dataset, the Ramos clones of Figure 7D, in which a single *Aicda*^{−/−} clone suppressed every call; it was then applied to the other datasets and was already fixed when the off-target libraries were analyzed, where it excluded no control; it excluded one in the mOrange analysis. It is therefore a rule derived from data rather than a pre-registered one, and it is reported here so that its effect can be judged. With fewer than two controls, or for indels, the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure[@benjamini1995] across positions and alternative alleles is used instead. The number of controls therefore selects between four modes, and the output records which was used in its `multiple_testing` field, s

140 heading :: What each mutation measure counts

141 para :: Three measures are reported per library, and they answer different questions; group tests then use one value per animal. A unique variant is a position and substituted base counted once per library, however many reads carry it; unique means distinct within that library and does not imply an independent mutational origin, since one lineage's trunk mutation is one unique variant. **Unique-variant density** is D / B × 10^{5}, where D is the number of unique variants and B the base observations at callable positions (inside the analysis window and the end trims), counted exactly as the read depth summed over those positions, not the number of reference positions. Because B grows with depth while D saturates, this density falls as depth rises even at a constant mutation rate, so it is compared only between libraries analyzed at the same depth (Figure S10). **Read-weighted mutation frequency** is Σ m_{i} / (N × L) × 10^{3}, where m_{i} is the number of called sites read i carries at or above the configured minimum base quality (0 for these read-library analyses), N the reads analyzed and L the window length; a mutation shared by many reads is counted once in each of them, so an expanded clone raises it. This fixed-window frequency credits every read with the whole window; an observed-base frequency, which counts only the bases each read covers, is reported beside it and agrees when reads span the window, as they did in the J_{H}4 and N114 V_{H} projects to within 2.4%. **Clone-weighted frequency** groups reads by their V(D)J junction, keeps a mutation when at least half the reads of its clone carry it (any read of a single-read clone), and is Σ c_{k} / (K × L) × 10^{3} over the K clones, so each clone counts once whatever its size. For wild-type J_{H}4 library Wt2 at 500 reads over the 529-bp window these give 221 unique variants over 263,886 base observations (83.8 × 10^{−5}), 747 read-level mutations over 264,500 bases (2.82 × 10^{−3}), and 207 clonal mutations over 24 clones (16.3 × 10^{−3}); the clone-weighted value is the largest because one clone holds 81% of the reads. For Sanger clones, where each clone is one molecule read once, the frequency denominator is the clones analyzed times L, with L the 529-bp window; the published studies used their own full consensus length and counted every filed clone, which is the convention Table S4 reports separately.

142 para :: Two implementation details of the error model. The rate used at a site is the pooled rate for that substitution, strand and quality over all window positions, used as a floor, with that position's own control count shrunk toward it by m = 500 pseudo-observations, so a position the controls show erring often keeps its high rate while a position that happened to show none does not look error-free. A substitution class the controls never showed is given half a pseudo-count over the control bases observed, which keeps the quality tail above zero rather than allowing a p value of exactly zero. Indels are called separately, because nanopore indel errors are systematic rather than quality-driven: each exact indel is tested against the rate of its class (insertion or deletion, length up to 4, homopolymer run up to 5), counted per read spanning each position of the analysis window and used as a floor, with that site's own control rate shrunk toward it and inflated by a margin fitted as the substitution margin is; candidates are corrected by Benjamini–Hochberg; indels are counted only at quality ≥ 30 at the inserted bases or the bases flanking a deletion, and those carried by fewer than three reads are not called. UMI consensus groups reads by their tags, allowing up to 3 edits per tag end, greedily from the most abundant tag and merging a neighbor only when it is at most half as abundant; groups of fewer than 3 reads are dropped, and each remaining group becomes one quality-weighted consensus read. The single-read estimate divides the excess of single-read substitutions at quality ≥ 35 over the controls' rate by the share of that sample's correctly read bases reaching that quality, so that the estimate does not depend on the cutoff; that it does not between Q30 and Q40 is the check reported for J_{H}4.

143 heading :: Single-read estimate, clones, mutation trees, and quality checks

144 para :: Single-read mutations not called individually are estimated in aggregate. SHMtrace counts Q≥35 single-read substitutions outside the accepted call set, subtracts the control expectation scaled to sample bases, and divides by the fraction of correctly read bases reaching that quality. Control events are counted only at positions and alleles where the sample could show a single-read event, so neither its per-site calls nor alleles it carries in two or more reads add to the background. The 95% interval inverts a profile likelihood in which the sample count is Poisson in signal plus background and the control count Poisson in background, scaled by the between-control overdispersion; it stays nonzero when neither sample nor controls show an event. In simulation it covered the true count in 92.5–98.8% of experiments with Poisson background, and in 87–90% when three heterogeneous controls carried several background events, because three controls estimate the overdispersion imprecisely. The combined density's interval holds the per-site calls fixed.

145 para :: Two different things are called a clone in this field, and this paper keeps them apart. A *Sanger clone* is one bacterial colony, that is one PCR product carried by one transformant and sequenced once: a unit of sequencing, which says nothing about which B cell the molecule came from, and two Sanger clones of one animal may well come from the same B cell lineage. A *B cell clone* is a lineage, defined here by a shared V(D)J junction, which is what the clone-weighted frequency and the mutation trees use. The two coincide only by accident. Where the text says a measure counts each clone once, it means the B cell clone; the Sanger denominator is clones analyzed × 529 bp, where those clones are the sequencing units. Sanger amplicons of the J_{H}4 intron do not span the junction, so B cell clones cannot be identified in them, and clone-weighted frequencies are reported only for the nanopore libraries.

146 para :: Junction grouping uses the twenty bases immediately upstream of alignment starts occurring within 18 bp of J_{H}4. Sequences are clustered greedily around abundance-ranked centroids with edit distance ≤3 and a free 5′ end. Each lineage's majority profile retains calls supported by at least half its reads. Tree nodes merge mutations supported by ≥2 lineage reads with Jaccard overlap ≥0.9; each node's parent is the smallest node containing ≥90% of its supporting reads. A read fits when its nodes occupy one ancestral path. The four-gamete test requires each of the four presence/absence combinations for a mutation pair in at least two reads.

147 para :: The targeting index is mean S5F mutability[@yaari2013] at mutated positions divided by the window mean. A one-sided permutation test uses 2,000 random position sets; the model was exported from SHazaM 1.2.0. Positional-clustering flags identify 10-base windows with more calls than expected under a Poisson distribution, with Bonferroni correction across windows (p < 10^{−3}). Figure S22 summarizes the workflow; Figure S23 illustrates the analytical rules using J_{H}4 libraries. Sanger traces are assigned through a sample sheet and trimmed to their longest reliably aligned Q≥20 segment. All Q≥20 substitutions are counted after germline filtering. Quality-control flags identify copied files, unexpectedly shared clones between animals (Poisson test weighted by mutation frequency across animals), excess mutations in controls, label mismatches and mutation-load outliers. Flags are reported for review without automatic exclusion. These flags were tested on forty simulated projects, each carrying one instance of every bookkeeping error: a changed label was detectable only where the affected clones shared mutations with another animal's clones, because a label alone leaves no trace in the sequence, and no correctly assigned clone was flagged in error among the 6,018 tested (`crm/sanger_validation_summary.json`).

148 para :: The Sanger route counts Q≥20 substitutions without per-call significance testing. AID-null clones provide the observed Q≥20 mismatch rate after their own germline differences are excluded, stratified by quality and position. Sites mismatched in more than one control clone are annotated on corresponding sample calls. This background estimate contextualizes Sanger counts but does not determine acceptance of individual substitutions.

Each treatment is analyzed separately against its own null controls at 20,000 reads per library. The repeat interval, positions 1–327, is reached by R2; a second analysis uses positions 20–240 in the twelve paired libraries. Its three Wt libraries represent one per treatment, so the pooled genotype contrast is descriptive rather than a balanced treatment-adjusted design (`crm/public_smu_pi3k.py`).

177 heading :: Quantification and statistical analysis

178 para :: Groups were compared with one value per library, each library being one animal, subclone, clone, culture or experiment as Table S2 states; reads were never treated as replicates. The Welch test on log_{10} rates is the primary test throughout, chosen before the analyses reported here; the negative-binomial and permutation tests are reported alongside it as sensitivity analyses, and Figure 3H compares all three. The negative-binomial test models each library's mutation count k with mean n·r, where n is that library's bases and r its group's rate, and variance n·r + φ(n·r)^{2} with one dispersion φ shared by both groups and fitted by maximum likelihood under the full model. No moderation or shrinkage across features is applied, since there is one endpoint. The statistic is the likelihood ratio against a common rate, and its null distribution comes from 499 parametric bootstrap samples drawn under that common rate with the fitted φ, because two to ten libraries per group are too few for the asymptotic χ^{2}. A group with no calls keeps rate zero rather than being floored. The permutation test is exact when the number of allocations allows, which sets a floor on the p value it can return: with three libraries per group there are only 20 allocations, so the smallest two-sided p is 0.10 and the test cannot reach 0.05 whatever the difference. Its apparent power of zero at n = 3 in Figure 3H is therefore arithmetic, not a property of the data, and it is why the Welch test is primary at these group sizes. Each cell of Figure 3H is 300 simulated experiments, so a reported share carries a Monte Carlo 95% interval no wider than ±5.6 percentage points (Wilson); under the null at n = 3 the Welch test rejected in 3.7% (95% CI 2.1–6.4) and the negative-binomial test in 5.0% (3.1–8.1), while pooling reads rejected in 91.3% (87.6–94.0). The Welch test uses log_{10} rates, with libraries without calls placed at their one-event density; a fold against such libraries therefore depends on that convention and is not a confidence bound. The same rule, one event on the library's own exposure, is used for every log-scale test, fold and plotted geometric mean; the software also reports each test with half an event and with a third of the smallest observed rate, as labelled sensitivity analyses, never as the primary result. The permutation test is exact. Folds are ratios of geometric means. Spectra were compared by pooled Pearson correlation across the 12 substitution types with a Fisher interval, by a repeated-measures test of the platform-by-substitution-type interaction with mouse as the matching factor, and by paired Šídák-adjusted t tests. Sample sizes, statistical tests, and dispersion measures are given in each figure legend. Analyses used Python 3.13 with NumPy and SciPy[@virtanen2020].
